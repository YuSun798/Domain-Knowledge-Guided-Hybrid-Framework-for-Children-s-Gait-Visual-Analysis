\begin{abstract}
This report presents our solution to the CVPR 2026 AI for Children Challenge (CV4CHL), which focuses on children's gait visual analysis from 2D keypoint sequences. The challenge comprises two tracks: Edinburgh Visual Gait Score (EVGS) prediction (Track~1) and gait pattern classification of Bilateral Spastic Cerebral Palsy (Track~2). We propose a domain-knowledge-guided hybrid framework that bridges clinical biomechanical expertise with data-driven machine learning. Our approach features five key components: (1)~a hierarchical data cleaning pipeline with double-pass temporal smoothing and per-joint outlier detection to handle inherently noisy pediatric keypoint data; (2)~clinically-informed feature engineering that maps keypoint sequences to biomechanically meaningful joint angles; (3)~a hybrid prediction strategy that fuses machine learning with rule-based clinical heuristics at the decision layer; (4)~cross-track knowledge transfer that leverages EVGS predictions as discriminative features for gait classification; and (5)~a cross-version ensemble mechanism that exploits prediction diversity from heterogeneous feature engineering pipelines. Through systematic experimentation across 14 solution versions, we derive 12 empirical laws governing small-sample clinical machine learning, revealing that data cleaning quality and domain knowledge integration are the primary drivers of generalization, while cross-validation and competition feedback can rank model variants differently under possible distribution shift. Our best recorded competition submission received a combined feedback score of 0.6664 under the then-used evaluation protocol; we distinguish this from the organizers' later standardized re-evaluation.
\end{abstract}
