\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a domain-knowledge-guided hybrid framework for the CVPR 2026 CV4CHL Challenge on children's gait visual analysis. Our approach demonstrates that on small clinical datasets, careful integration of domain knowledge at the right abstraction layer---specifically, prediction-level fusion rather than feature-level encoding---can substantially outperform purely data-driven methods. The hierarchical data cleaning pipeline proves to be the single most important component, highlighting the critical role of preprocessing when training and test distributions diverge.

Through systematic ablation across 14 solution versions, we derived 12 empirical laws that characterize the behavior of machine learning in small-sample clinical settings. Notable findings include the anti-correlation between cross-validation and test performance under distribution shift, the superiority of hardcoded clinical rules over data-fitted rules when training samples are scarce, and the risk of naively combining individually effective methods. These insights extend beyond the current challenge and may guide future work in medical AI where data scarcity is prevalent.

Our analysis reveals that Track~1 (EVGS scoring) accounts for 97\% of the remaining performance gap, suggesting that handcrafted feature engineering approaches have inherent limitations for fine-grained kinematic scoring. Future directions include exploring end-to-end deep learning architectures such as Spatial-Temporal Graph Convolutional Networks~\cite{yan2018spatial} and Transformer-based sequence models that can learn discriminative temporal patterns directly from raw keypoint sequences, as well as multi-task learning frameworks that jointly optimize EVGS scoring and gait classification objectives.
